# Phase 5 — ATX Power Supply Integration

The ATX PSU needs a permanent on/off mechanism since it is no longer controlled by a motherboard. The PC case's existing power button is re-wired to control the PSU directly.

## 5a — Replace the Momentary Switch with a Latching Push Button

Standard PC power buttons are **momentary** (press and release = pulse). To keep the ATX PSU powered on, you need a **latching** button (press = ON, press again = OFF).

1. Locate the power switch connector on the front panel cable bundle of the PC case.

2. Desolder or disconnect the existing momentary push button from the case's power switch header.

3. Solder in a new **latching push button** in its place, using shrink tubing over each solder joint.

4. Route the two wires from this button to a **terminal block** for a cleaner connection to the ATX connector.

## 5b — Wire PS_ON to the Latching Button

1. On the ATX 24-pin connector, identify:
   - **PS_ON** — Pin 16 (green wire)
   - **GND** — Any black wire (e.g., Pin 15 or 17)

2. Cut or tap these two wires (do not cut near the PSU — work near the connector end).

3. Connect the **PS_ON** wire and a **GND** wire to the two terminals of the latching push button (via terminal blocks for a clean connection).

4. When the button is latched closed (ON position), it shorts PS_ON to GND, signalling the ATX PSU to power on. When released (OFF), the PSU shuts down.

> ℹ️ **How ATX Power-On Works:** An ATX PSU remains in standby until its PS_ON pin is pulled to GND (below ~0.8V). Shorting PS_ON to GND via the button mimics what a motherboard does when you press power.
